## H. Calculations

Each line names its inputs and their status. The layout study (`v2/05-Engineering/layout-study/`) and the sound and air check (`v2/05-Engineering/acoustics-air/`) replace these first estimates with their own scripts.

- **C1 Conversions (calculation).** 1 in = 25.4 mm exactly. 48 × 40 in = 1219.2 × 1016.0 mm. 48 × 96 in = 1219.2 × 2438.4 mm. 6 ft = 1828.8 mm. 36 × 80 in = 914.4 × 2032.0 mm. 4.25 m² = 45.7 sq ft (1 m² = 10.764 sq ft). Rounded to whole mm in the tables.
- **C2 CO2 at steady state (calculation).** Rise above the supply air: ΔC = n·G / (Q·ε). Inputs: n = 2 people; G = 0.0052 l/s of CO2 per seated adult (assumption, a typical value); supply air at 400 ppm, near outdoor level (assumption); mixing effectiveness ε = 1 (perfect mixing) or the PM's 0.8 (D-52). For CO2 ≤ 800 ppm, ΔC ≤ 400 ppm, so Q ≥ 2 × 0.0052 / 0.000400 = **26 l/s**. In a 600 ppm office (PLAN Target 4, D-43), ΔC ≤ 200 ppm, so Q ≥ 52 l/s with perfect mixing. That is why two people get 53 l/s. One person makes half the CO2, so 26 l/s holds 800 ppm for one person in a 600 ppm office with perfect mixing; that is the floor while someone is inside (D-44). At ε = 0.8 the same office needs 0.0052 / (0.000200 × 0.8) = **about 33 l/s** for one person and **65 l/s** for two, the boost (D-52); at the 26 l/s floor one person sits at about 850 ppm, so the CO2 control raises the flow (HW §7a). At the 53 l/s normal flow (D-33), ΔC = 0.0104 / 53 = 196 ppm, so CO2 ≈ **596 ppm** in a 400 ppm room, or **796 ppm** in a 600 ppm office (845 ppm at ε = 0.8). At the 65 l/s boost, ΔC = 160 ppm, so CO2 ≈ 760 ppm in a 600 ppm office (800 ppm at ε = 0.8). Office air is often above 400 ppm; the pod then sits higher by the same amount.

- **C4 Heat (calculation).** Temperature rise ΔT = P / (ρ · c_p · Q), with ρ = 1.2 kg/m³ and c_p = 1005 J/(kg·K) (air at about 20 °C, source: standard values). Heat inputs, from the air report's model with ELECTRICAL's loads (PR #72, from ELECTRICAL's PR #64): 2 seated people at 75 W sensible each and 2 laptops at 30 W each (assumptions), the work scene's 22 W of lights (the shelf uplight dimmed and the down-light; the call light is off) and the supplies' losses 8.4 W (calculation, ELECTRICAL), the hub 3 W (assumption, ISS-060), and the two supply fans about 5.2 W at 53 l/s (cube law; the maker's figure is unverified), so P = 249 W (240 W before ELECTRICAL's loads; 257 W in PR #66 with the lights at full). At the 53 l/s normal flow: ΔT = 249 / (1.2 × 1005 × 0.053) = **3.9 K**, so 53 l/s holds D-50's 4 K limit; 4 K takes 51 l/s for two people and 29 l/s for one (the model, PR #72). One person at the 26 l/s floor reaches about 4.4 K and two people at 26 l/s about 7.8 K, one more reason they get 53 l/s (D-44). At the 65 l/s boost, two people sit at about 3.2 K. The voice kit at D-88's 40 W average with 8.2 W of supply loss gives 297 W at 53 l/s, 4.6 K; 4 K takes 62 l/s (28.7 dB(A)), inside the 65 l/s boost, where the inside is 3.83 K over the room (PR #72). So the flow above the CO2 minimum, and the boost, are what keep the pod cool, which matters because heat is the users' top complaint (MS).
